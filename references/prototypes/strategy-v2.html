02

The hard limits, and how v2 designs around them

Four of your asks run into legal, licensing or privacy walls. None kills the idea. Each changes how we build it.

GMAIL INBOX

Reading mail needs Google's security assessment

Sending only (gmail.send) is a "sensitive" scope: Google verification, no paid audit. Reading threads needs a "restricted" scope, which triggers a CASA security assessment. It's paid (roughly $675 to $5,400 depending on level), renewed every year, and can take weeks to months. Until then: 100-user cap and a warning screen.

  • v1: compose link (no permissions)
  • v1.5: send-only scope + replies marked by hand or via a BCC tracking address
  • v2: read-only investor threads after CASA, once paying users justify it
CRUNCHBASE

No scraping. A licence, or our own sources.

Crunchbase's terms bar automated collection and resale; the API is paid and needs a commercial licence to show data inside our product. Same for Tracxn and OpenVC.

  • Now: competitors' own websites, public funding news (Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory feeds), founder-added competitors, AI summaries with links
  • Later: price a Crunchbase or Tracxn API licence once revenue covers it
FACES & LOGOS

Logos yes, faces only with consent

A fund's logo from its own website is fine to show as identification. Investors' personal photos from LinkedIn or elsewhere are not ours to reuse, and DPDP Act consent rules apply to personal data.

  • Funds: logo or favicon from their domain, with a generated mark as fallback
  • People: initials until they claim a profile and upload a photo
PUBLIC LISTINGS

A "raising now" leaderboard can look like a public offer

Indian private placements go to a limited, pre-identified group (at most 200 a year), and SEBI has acted against platforms that turned private placements into public offers. So a Product Hunt-style public board of who's raising is risky.

  • Public boards rank investors (responsiveness, activity), never offers
  • "Most saved startups" and round details are visible only to verified investors, opt-in for founders
  • We never handle money, allotments or terms. Get a securities lawyer's view before launching the investor side.

Also: the DPDP Act 2023 applies to investor emails and founder data. We need a privacy notice, a purpose for every field, a way for any investor to correct or remove their record, and deletion on request.

03

Earning a "vetted investors" number

RoundFunded says "60,000+ vetted". A big number we can't explain is a liability. Ours is smaller, defined, and shown on every record.

◌ RESEARCHED

Sourced from public pages

Thesis, stages, cheque and portfolio from the investor's own site, with dated links. This is what last night's 148-investor run produced.

◐ CHECKED

Reviewed by our team

A person confirms the fund is active (a dated deal in the last 12 months), the pitch route works, and the facts match the sources. It expires after 90 days.

● CLAIMED

Confirmed by the investor

The investor signs in with their work email and confirms their thesis. This is the strongest trust signal, and it ranks higher for founders.

WEEKLY

Discover. New fund names from SEBI's AIF register, SIDBI's Fund of Funds list, angel network pages and funding news. Removes duplicates against what we have.

WEEKLY

Research. The same agent run as last night, at about $0.12 per investor on Sonnet. Facts without a source stay blank.

DAILY

Check. One person checks about 40 records a day from a queue, in roughly 2 minutes each. Low-confidence and high-traffic investors come first.

ALWAYS

Signals. Founder outcomes (bounces, replies, "not investing") feed back into freshness. A bounced email drops a record back to Researched.

The public number is "Checked in the last 90 days". Target: 300 by launch, 1,000 by month 3. That's honest, explainable, and it grows every week.

04

The investor side, kept as simple as possible

Investors join for one reason: fewer, better-fitting founders. Onboarding is three taps, because we've already done the research.

STEP 1

Sign in with work email (Google Workspace or a sign-in link). If the email's domain matches a fund we researched, the claim is verified on the spot. Angels with personal email get one extra step: a LinkedIn URL plus a manual check, or a vouch from a claimed investor.

STEP 2

Confirm the thesis. Sectors, stages and cheque are pre-filled as chips; tap to fix. It takes 30 seconds, and it's the data that makes matching better for everyone.

STEP 3

Door policy. Maximum intros a week, what founders must include, and how close a fit to show. We enforce it on the founder side, which is why investors trust it.

What investors get

  • A deal feed matched to their thesis, opt-in founders only
  • Data rooms with a readiness score
  • "Pass with a reason" in one tap
  • Their own responsiveness stats and badge

What we get

  • Verified, current thesis data (the moat)
  • Reply and outcome data
  • A reason for founders to pay: claimed investors who actually read

06

Competition & news: the workflow

SEED

From her company name, website and one-liner, the AI proposes 5–8 competitors. She keeps, removes or adds.

ENRICH

For each competitor: website summary, funding rounds from public news, backers (cross-checked with our investor database), team size range. Every cell links to its source.

CONNECT

Backers of competitors are flagged in her matches. Investors who backed adjacent companies are boosted.

WATCH

A weekly news scan on her sector, competitors, matched investors and policy, ranked High or Medium for her pitch, each with "how to use this".

USE

Her "how you differ" lines feed the email drafts and the data room's competition slide.

INDIA DOCS

Readiness score: story 25, proof 35, documents 25, completeness 15. Plus a viewer list, and "10 questions investors will ask", generated from her gaps.

08

Moats: what gets harder to copy every month

STRONGEST

1 · Outcome data

Who replied, how fast, who took meetings, who passed and why. Nobody else in India has reply and outcome data per investor. It makes the Fit Score better and powers the responsiveness rankings, and it compounds with every email sent.

STRONGEST

2 · Claimed investor profiles

Once investors maintain their thesis and door policy here, our data is fresher than any scraped directory, and founders come because investors are here.

3 · Sourced, dated India dataset

Every fact linked and dated, checked by a person, with India-specific fields (DPIIT, SIDBI-backed funds, ₹ cheques, cities). Hard to fake, slow to copy.

4 · Workflow lock-in

Pipeline, inbox, data room and competition table in one place. Leaving means rebuilding the whole raise.

5 · Distribution you already own

GrowthX, IIT/IIM/IFC networks, incubators and the trainers we found. Founders arrive through trusted cohorts, not ads.

6 · Trust as brand

"Nothing sent without you", "every claim sourced", "India's rules": in a market wary of spam and shady listing platforms, that's a position, not a feature.

10

How we win India

Wedge

First-time founders raising pre-seed or seed (₹50L–₹5Cr) in AI, Climate and Deeptech. We own that corner before going broad: the best data for one corner beats average data for all.

First 100 founders

GrowthX Build Sprint cohorts, your IIT Delhi, FMS, IFC and Enactus network (200–300 reachable this week), and 5 incubators offering it free to their cohorts.

Channels that compound

  • Incubator licences: Startup India-recognised incubators and IIT/IIM cells get cohort seats
  • Trainers as partners: the coaches we listed get a referral share
  • Content engine: a weekly "Most responsive investors in India" post on LinkedIn
  • Investor-side pull: every claimed investor tells founders "pitch me here"

Pricing (hypothesis, to test)

  • Founders: free list + 5 emails; then about ₹1,499–2,999 a month during an active raise (raises are seasonal, so offer a pause)
  • Incubators: per-cohort licence
  • Investors: always free (they're the supply)
  • Later: success-based data room or intro credits, never a cut of the round

North-star metrics

Founder replies per 10 emails sent (quality of matching and writing) and meetings booked per founder per month (the job done). Secondary: checked investors, claimed investors, weekly active founders during a raise.
