## 1. The job

When **my runway is down to 3–4 months and my investor pipeline is thin or going quiet**, I want **to know exactly who to write to next, with the email ready**, so I can **get investor meetings booked without doing the research and writing myself**.

Other moments it happens (the little hire: each time she sits down to work the raise):
- A warm conversation has gone quiet and I haven't followed up
- An investor passes and I need the next name
- I've decided to raise and have no system and a thin warm network (the big hire, once)
- An investor asks for a deck, FAQ or DD folder and I have nothing ready (parked: not this sprint)

Who, by situation: a founder raising a first round (₹50L to ₹10Cr) while running the company, with a thin warm network, who has already tried parts of it by hand (a Sheet, cold emails, ChatGPT drafts). **[guess, review]**

Today they hire: Google and a Sheets or Notion tracker, emails written one by one, ChatGPT for single drafts, warm intros, Tracxn or Venture Intelligence for data, or an advisor at ₹50,000–2,00,000/month. For many, nothing.

One person: the founder is user, decision maker and payer. Built for this weekend: the founder.

(Advanced) What needs doing: know who to write to, write it well, send it, follow up on time.
(Advanced) How they want to feel: in control of a process, not waiting on intros. **[guess, review]**
(Advanced) How they want to look to others: more prepared than any founder the investor met that week.

## 2. The switch

What they'd fire: the late-night Googling, the from-scratch emails, the Sheets tracker and the forgotten follow-ups. For some, the advisor retainer.

The forces that matter:
- **Push:** the runway clock is running, there is no pipeline, and months have already gone on investors who were never a fit. The deeper push is probably the realisation that her own ad hoc process is why nothing is moving. **[guess, review: test in the two founder interviews]**
- **Anxiety:** an email goes to an investor in her name and it is generic, wrong or spammy, and a first impression with an investor can't be undone. She has likely seen ChatGPT's generic drafts already. **[guess, review]**
- **Habit:** warm intros, a Sheets tracker and ChatGPT for a draft. Slow and hit or miss, but free and in her control.

(Pull left out on purpose: "close faster" is the job. Most builders only pitch pull.)

What the product does about each:
- Push -> within minutes she has 20 investors worth writing to and 3 emails ready, so a pipeline exists tonight.
- Anxiety -> it runs on her own company, not a demo. Every investor shows why they match. Every email is hers to edit and send; nothing leaves without her. One plain line where she enters her details says what happens to them.
- Habit -> she sends from her own Gmail, can export the tracker to a Sheet, and doesn't have to stop asking friends for intros. Starting costs four fields.

The one worry onboarding must remove: "What if it sends something wrong to an investor in my name?" **[guess, review: rewrite in the words a founder actually used]**

## 3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before your product). Neha, a first-time founder of a seed-stage B2B startup, 2-person team, raising ₹2Cr **[guess, review: swap in a real founder you know]**:
1. Tuesday, 11 PM. She checks the bank balance: 3.5 months of runway. The investor tab in her Sheet has six names she Googled.
2. She Googles who invests in her sector
3. She asks founder friends for intros (hit or miss)
4. She researches each investor one by one and updates the Sheet
5. She writes a cold email from scratch, or asks ChatGPT and rewrites it
6. She sends it and tracks replies by hand
7. She follows up when she remembers
8. A warm conversation goes cold
9. Three to six months pass with no meeting that matters. Not done.

With my product (happy path; her steps and the product's steps marked):
1. Tuesday, 11 PM. She checks the bank balance: 3.5 months of runway. (Her step)
2. She opens the link from a founder group and enters company name, sector, stage and ask size. (Her step)
3. She sees 20 investors ranked, each with one line on why they fit, and the top 3 emails written to them. (Product's step)
4. She reads one, changes a line, taps "Open in Gmail", and sends it. (Her step)
5. She marks it "sent". (Her step)
6. Five days later she sees "3 follow-ups due" with each one written, and sends them the same way. (Product's step, then hers)
7. An investor replies. She taps "replied" and that investor's follow-ups stop. (Her step)
8. A meeting is booked. The job is done: she is talking to investors who fit, instead of searching for them.

Things it takes to get the job done today: 9 · With my product: 4 (enter details, review and send, mark replies, take the meeting)

What must not happen:
- Step 3: an investor who doesn't invest at her stage, sector or geography is in her top 20.
- Step 3: an email states something about an investor or their portfolio that isn't true.
- Step 4: anything is sent that she hasn't read.
- Step 6: a follow-up is written for an investor who already replied.
- Any step: she closes the tab and loses her list and statuses.

What goes wrong, numbered by step (full table with what happens and where it rejoins: `product-thinking/03-core-flow.md`):
- 2a: sector matches no investors in the seed list -> say so, offer the 3 closest sectors. Must-have.
- 3a: fewer than 20 fit -> show what fits, never pad the list. Must-have.
- 3b: no answer in 15 seconds -> say so, keep her details, offer a retry. Must-have.
- 4a: the Gmail link is too long or doesn't open -> copy the email to her clipboard instead. Must-have.
- 6a: investor replied on WhatsApp or LinkedIn -> she marks "replied" by hand. Must-have.
- 7a: investor passes -> mark "passed" and suggest 3 next names. Can wait.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered):
- Coming back: she opens the tracker, sees who is due a follow-up or reply, and sends. This is where she returns.

## 4. Onboarding

First value: her own company, with 20 named investors, a reason for each, and an email to the top one that already reads like her.

The smallest commitment we ask for: company name, sector, stage and ask size, and nothing more. No deck, no Gmail connection, no sign-up.

The worry it removes: "What if it sends something wrong to an investor in my name?" **[guess, review: replace with a founder's own words after the interviews]**

From opening the link to the first value:
1. She opens the link a friend or the founder group shared. removes: "another tool to learn"
2. She sees four fields and one button, with "Used only to match investors and write your drafts. Nothing is sent without you." removes: "what does this do with my name and my numbers?"
3. She enters her details and sees her own 20, a reason for each, and the first email. (first value) removes: "will it understand early-stage Indian investors?"
4. She edits the email and opens it in Gmail. Nothing has been sent until she presses send. removes: "what if it's wrong?"
5. She saves her list and marks the email sent. removes: "will I lose this?"

Login: not before step 3. Asked at step 5, when she wants to keep her list, because saving it is the reason to sign up.
What we don't ask on day one: deck, DD documents, Drive, Gmail permission, LinkedIn, profile, tour.
What we ask later, and when: a one-line description of the company after her first email is sent, to sharpen later drafts; her deck after a meeting is booked (parked, not this sprint).

How we count it (details: `product-thinking/04-onboarding.md`): Setup = submitted the four details. Aha = saw her 20 and the first email. Habit = came back on a second day and sent a follow-up or added investors within 7 days. A step log in Convex records only the step name and time: no names, no emails, no company details.

## 5. v1

**Does** (the must haves: one person finishes the one job):
- Four fields in, 20 ranked investors out from a seeded list of about 100, each with a reason
- A personalised email for each investor, written to that investor's thesis
- Edit the email and open it prefilled in her Gmail to send; copy to clipboard if the link fails
- A tracker with a status per investor: not contacted, sent, replied, meeting, passed
- Follow-ups drafted and shown when due, stopped by "replied"
- Her list, edits and statuses saved when she closes and reopens
- The must-have branches: no sector match (2a), fewer than 20 fits (3a), no answer in 15 seconds (3b), Gmail link fails (4a), reply on another channel marked by hand (6a)

**Doesn't** (not this sprint: parked, not forgotten):
- Pitch narrative, FAQ and DD folder
- Investor update emails and the DD Q&A agent
- A live investor tracker with deployment signals and portfolio moves
- LinkedIn, WhatsApp agent, Outlook and Google Drive
- Sending automatically from her inbox or reading her replies (needs Gmail API approval)
- Login before first value, deck upload, a tour, a profile
- Anything that sends without her pressing send

**Nice to have** (only after the must haves work):
- Skip an investor she already knows (3c), "rewrite with this note" (4b), "passed" suggests 3 next names (7a), odd ask-size warning (2b)
- Import her existing Sheet; export the tracker to a Sheet
- Weekly digest of new investors to add; WhatsApp reminder when follow-ups are due
- Comparable recent rounds mentioned inside the drafts
- Gmail API send and reply detection

**How I'll know it worked** (what they do again, not what they say): of the first 10 founders who see their list, at least 4 open an email in Gmail within 24 hours, at least 3 return within 7 days to send a follow-up or add investors, and at least 1 gets a reply to an email the product wrote. **[guess, review: numbers are a choice, set your own]**

Build first for the use case with the biggest pain: a first-time founder with 4 months of runway or less. The seed list covers the 2–3 sectors where you know the investors best, not all of India. **[guess, review: which sectors]**

## 6. The riskiest guess

Imagined failure: "It's next Friday, and no founder used it twice. Why?" The reasons, in the order I would fear them: the drafts sound generic and founders won't send them; the matched investors are wrong or stale; founders don't trust an AI to write to investors; they prefer warm intros and ignore cold outreach; investors don't reply to cold email anyway; founders won't come back after the first list.

If this is false, the product is pointless: founders will send an email an AI wrote to a real investor, and it is good enough that investors reply. **[guess, review]**

Decided before the test:
- We believe: a founder will send an AI-drafted investor email with only light edits.
- To check, we will: build, for 3 real founders, 10 matched investors and 2 drafts each by hand from the seed list, show them on WhatsApp, and ask "would you send this today?"
- And measure: how many drafts each founder would send, what they edit, and how many I would send myself.
- We're right if: at least 4 of the 6 drafts shown would be sent with under two minutes of edits, and I would send at least 5 of 6 myself.

Thirty-minute check, no code (run it while you build), and what happened:
- **Not run yet.** Best guess before running (a prediction, not a result): founders will send the top draft with edits but reject drafts that name a portfolio company wrongly; "sounds like me" will matter more than the investor list. **[guess, review: replace with what actually happened]**
- If it fails: Sections 4 and 5 change, because the drafts become the product and need a review step; the list becomes secondary.
- Do the AI part by hand first: for 3 days, three founders send their four details on WhatsApp and I reply with 10 investors and an email. Only use founders who'd be fine finding out it was me. **[guess, review]**

## 7. Milestones

1. I can enter one real company and read 3 emails to real investors that I would send myself (the investors hardcoded)
2. I can see 20 investors ranked from a seeded list of 100, a reason for each, and a retry message if it takes over 15 seconds
3. I can edit an email and open it prefilled in my Gmail, or copy it if the link fails
4. I can enter a sector with no match and get a clear message, not a junk list
5. I can mark an investor "sent" and see it in the tracker
6. I can see follow-ups drafted for investors I emailed five days ago
7. I can mark a reply and that investor's follow-ups stop
8. A stranger can finish the whole flow without me explaining it, and the step log shows each step reached
Last: I can close it, reopen it, and my data is still there.
